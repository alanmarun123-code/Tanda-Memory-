Methodology & ethics

How the archive
is made.

The archive combines source-based curation with a participatory model. The distinction between collected information and community testimony is maintained throughout.

01 · Scope

A small archive with a clear boundary.

The assessment requires 10–15 curated items. Tanda Memory contains 13 initial items so the collection remains substantial without pretending to be exhaustive.

The collection covers cultural practices, history, language, belief, mobility, social life, and craftsmanship. Three of the thirteen entries focus specifically on craftsmanship; craftsmanship is intentionally not positioned as the entire definition of the community.

02 · Source distinction

Different kinds of evidence stay visible.

Public-source material is compiled from existing documentation and is identified as such. Community narratives are treated differently: they are submitted by contributors, contextualised, and held for review before publication.

This prevents a public website from quietly turning an outsider's description, a secondary source, and a community member's personal memory into the same kind of evidence.

Community-based participation

Consent

Participation begins with permission.

Community participants were contacted through the Karnataka Pradesh Banjara Lambani Seva Sangha. Contributors can choose how their names are represented; where anonymity is requested, the archive does not publish their names.

Review

Publication is not automatic.

Anyone can submit a narrative, but submission does not equal publication. A narrative remains pending until it has been considered through the community review process. The aim is to give community members a role in deciding what enters the public archive.

Context

One account is not everyone.

Personal testimony is identified as a contributor's account. The archive avoids converting one person's experience into a universal statement about all Lambani/Banjara communities, especially where practices vary by region, family, or context.

Images

No unconsented photographs.

The site contains no photographs. Since community members were not comfortable sharing images, the project does not scrape photographs from the internet or use stock imagery to stand in for them.

Copyright, attribution, and licensing

Existing material remains attributed to its original source rather than being presented as original research. The archive should link back to sources wherever possible and should not reproduce copyrighted text beyond what is necessary for identification or fair quotation. Community submissions remain connected to their contributor and should only be published under terms that the contributor understands and accepts.

For the submitted version of the project, source-specific copyright and licence information should be retained on the Sources page and alongside any material that is reproduced or adapted.

AI usage

AI tools are used as technical and editorial assistance: organising notes, refining interface language, drafting structural copy, and helping write website code. AI is not treated as a community participant, fieldwork source, or authority on Lambani/Banjara culture. Community testimony is not generated by AI and should not be represented as such.

Publication workflow

01 · Submit

A contributor sends a narrative, correction, memory, or cultural account through the Interaction page.

02 · Review

The submission is checked for consent, attribution, sensitivity, context, copyright, and whether publication is appropriate.

03 · Community approval

Community review determines whether the narrative should enter the public collection.

Ethical principle

The archive values continuity without turning cultural life into a fixed display. Correction and disagreement are part of the archive rather than failures of it.

Assessment alignment

Archival gap and continuity, curation and organisation, ethical collection, source attribution, community participation, copyright awareness, accessibility, and AI acknowledgement are built into the site structure.

Important distinction. A static website can demonstrate the submission and approval workflow, but a live public archive should connect the form to a secure database and authenticated community moderation system before public deployment.
