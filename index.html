Tanda
Memory

A digital archive of cultural memory, everyday knowledge, histories, practices, and stories connected to Lambani/Banjara life.

Archive principle. This archive is designed to collect and organise cultural material without claiming to speak for an entire community. Public-source material is identified as such, while community narratives are treated as contributions that require consent, contextualisation, and community review before publication.
Welcome

A record that remains open.

Tanda Memory responds to an archival gap: cultural knowledge and lived experiences of Lambani/Banjara communities are often dispersed across craft documentation, historical accounts, institutional sources, and outsider representations rather than gathered through a community-sensitive digital archive.

The archive therefore combines carefully attributed existing sources with a participatory space where community members and other contributors can submit narratives. A submission is not automatically treated as archival fact. Publication is a separate process involving review and community approval.

13 curated items. One living archive.

The current collection contains thirteen artefacts or cultural items. Three entries focus on craftsmanship; the remaining entries explore other aspects of cultural life, including settlement, language, belief, festivals, oral memory, mobility, and social practices. No photographs are used because community members were not comfortable sharing images.

How to use the archive

01 · Explore

Read the curated collection and follow the categories to see how material culture sits alongside language, belief, history, memory, and contemporary life.

02 · Question

Read the methodology and source notes to understand where an entry came from, what kind of evidence it represents, and why it is included.

03 · Contribute

Use the Interaction page to submit a memory, story, correction, interpretation, or cultural narrative. Publication happens only after review.

Archive statement

No photographs are included in the present archive. The design uses pattern, colour, typography, and text rather than images of community members or cultural objects.

AI-usage acknowledgement. AI tools were used to assist with organising, editing, interface writing, and website code. AI-generated wording is not treated as community testimony or as an independent source of cultural authority.
