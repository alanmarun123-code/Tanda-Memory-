Where the
record comes from.

The archive separates public-source research from community contribution so readers can see how each kind of knowledge enters the collection.

Source policy

Attribution is part of the archive.

The initial collection is assembled from public and research-oriented sources rather than presented as original field discovery.

Source types represented in the research include craft archives and institutional material, academic and historical writing, linguistic and cultural sources, Wikipedia for background orientation, and recent press. Specific citations should be attached to the final submission wherever a claim is taken from a particular source.

Gaatha and craft documentation

Used as part of the craft-oriented research surrounding Lambani textile and embroidery practices.

Global InCH

Used as a craft and intangible cultural heritage reference in the research base.

Sandur Kushala Kala Kendra

Institutional and craft-related material used to contextualise textile traditions and making practices.

Academic and historical sources

Used for background on Banjara/Lambani history, movement, language, cultural identity, and social practices.

Wikipedia and reference material

Used only as a background orientation source where appropriate, not as a substitute for stronger primary or academic sources.

Recent press and public reporting

Used to identify contemporary discussions and representations. Press material remains attributed to the original publication.

Community participants

Community participants were contacted through the Karnataka Pradesh Banjara Lambani Seva Sangha. Participant names are not published where anonymity was requested. Community narratives are distinguished from compiled public-source entries.

Attribution model

Public source

Compiled material

Material gathered from existing sources is labelled as compiled material. The archive does not imply that the student created the underlying knowledge.

Community source

Contributor narrative

Community submissions are attributed according to the contributor's chosen level of identification and are published only after the required review.

Copyright and licensing note

The archive avoids reproducing photographs and does not use images of community members or cultural objects. For text and other copyrighted material, the final submitted archive should link to the original source, use only what is necessary for documentation, and record licence information wherever the source provides it.

AI-usage acknowledgement

AI tools assisted in the organisation of the website, drafting of interface copy, editing for clarity, and generation of code. AI is not cited as an authority for cultural claims. The distinction between public-source material and community testimony remains explicit, and no AI-generated narrative is presented as a participant's voice.

Source responsibility

A source list is not only a bibliography. It tells readers how knowledge entered the archive and makes the archive accountable for its representations.

Community responsibility

The archive's strongest ethical commitment is that community participation is not decorative. It is part of the publication process.
