About the archive

Why this
archive exists.

A counter-archive does not simply add more information to an existing collection. It asks who has been recorded, who has been left out, and who gets to decide how cultural memory is represented.

Archival gap

From absence to participation.

The archive begins from the recognition that Lambani/Banjara cultural life is documented in fragments: craft catalogues, historical writing, linguistic sources, institutional records, media accounts, and other public materials.

Those fragments are valuable, but they can also position the community primarily as an object of documentation. Tanda Memory changes the emphasis by placing attribution, context, consent, and future community contribution at the centre of the archive.

Purpose

This project is designed as a digital counter-archive emerging from an earlier counter-archive proposal. Its purpose is to bring together a small, curated body of cultural material while creating a mechanism through which people connected to the community can add their own narratives.

The archive is not intended to be an encyclopaedia or a definitive account of Lambani/Banjara identity. It is deliberately limited in scope: thirteen curated items form the initial collection, and the interaction system keeps the archive open to correction, supplementation, disagreement, and new memories.

Intended audience

Community

For people whose memories are being recorded

The archive provides a place for community members to see how cultural material is represented, submit their own narratives, identify inaccuracies, and participate in decisions about publication.

Researchers & students

For readers who need context

Students, researchers, educators, and interested readers can use the archive as a starting point for understanding cultural practices while following source attribution and methodological notes.

What makes it a counter-archive?

The project moves away from a model where an outside collector gathers, interprets, and permanently fixes a community's identity. Instead, it distinguishes between compiled public material and community contribution, makes the limits of the collection visible, avoids unconsented imagery, and gives contributors a route into the archival process.

The archive is not presented as the final word. It is presented as a record that can be questioned, corrected, expanded, and reinterpreted.

Scope

The initial archive contains 13 curated items and focuses on cultural memory, practices, material culture, belief, language, history, mobility, and everyday life.

Continuity

The collection is the first layer. Community submissions create the possibility of an archive that can continue beyond the assessment project.
