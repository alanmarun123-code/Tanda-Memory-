Interaction

A living archive needs living voices.

The Interaction page is the participatory layer of Tanda Memory. It creates a route for people to contribute material that may not appear in conventional archives.

Contributors can submit in their own words. They can also identify themselves as anonymous. The archive's stated goal is not to collect as much material as possible, but to make responsible space for narratives that contributors are comfortable placing into an archival record.

Submit a narrative

What happens next?

  1. The submission enters the review queue.
  2. Consent, attribution, sensitivity, and context are considered.
  3. The community review process decides whether it is appropriate to publish.
  4. If approved, the narrative can become part of the public archive.

Privacy: Do not submit personal information you do not want included in an academic archive. If you want your contribution to be anonymous, leave the name field blank.

Photographs: This archive currently does not request photographs.

Community review queue

In the submitted demonstration version, this area shows how narratives move from pending material toward publication. The browser stores demonstration submissions locally; a real public deployment should use a secure server-side database and authenticated community moderators.

Why participation matters

A participatory archive treats contributors not simply as sources of raw data but as people with a stake in how their knowledge is framed. The review model therefore gives community members a role in deciding whether a narrative is ready to enter the public record.

For contributors

Your account can be personal, family-based, historical, corrective, or reflective. The archive welcomes narratives that help complicate or deepen what is already present.

For reviewers

Approval should consider consent, attribution, context, potential harm, copyright, and whether the contribution is appropriate for public archival publication.

AI-usage acknowledgement. AI can assist with editing submitted material only where the contributor and review process permit it. AI should never be used to fabricate testimony, quotations, or community approval.
